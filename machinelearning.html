  • Project Statement

      Goals

      • Deepen our understanding of how machine learning (ML) technology works
      • Experience the challenges that come with ML technologies
      • Understand the importance of knowing one’s audience when designing ML models
      • Dialog with Buolamwini’s book Unmasking AI and her concept of “the coded gaze” in relation to our ML Model

      Deepen our understanding of how machine learning (ML) technology works

      It is one thing to learn about ML technology and another to try to use it. By putting our knowledge to practice, we gain a more comprehensive perspective of the technology that is becoming more common with every passing day.

      While this project does not require us to know all of the probabilistic equations behind ML, it does give us a taste of what makes them work well. For example, we have not only read about but have now also seen how ML relies heavily on the quality of this training data for its results. Data quality can make or break a ML model.

      Experience the challenges that come with ML technologies

      We have designed our model to recognize x different types of silverware.

      Josiah

      In an initial test, I (Josiah) realized just how significant the background was and is for our model. When I turned my computer camera from one perspective to another, the probable result changed to correspond with the background much more than the silverware that I had put in front of the camera. Background matters.

      I then tried to neutralize the issue by creating a rigged up green screen using a green sweater I have. However, I still experienced challenges. When showing my classmates my test model with the “green screen,” one of the silverware (I think it was the dinner knife) simply would not be recognized by the machine. Again, I think background was playing a significant part in prohibiting the effectiveness of the model

      My takeaway is that the ML learning model really needs a wide array of backgrounds to truly be accurate.

      Understand the importance of knowing one’s audience when designing ML models

      The readings assigned in LIS 500 and my (Josiah’s) experience of the background challenge has in part if not in whole, made me realize the importance of knowing the ML model’s audience. In AI vs. Human Creativity from Australia's ABC films, I think the AI comedy routine may have failed in part because it did not have enough information about the audience. In our case, it would be nice for the model to recognize silverware from around the world, but that goal is simply too big for the scope of this assignment. What we think of as traditional silverware may be totally different in another part of the world. This does not mean we should give up but that we will need to update the ML model’s data as its audience expands.

      Extrapolating on this point, would it be better to create a unique ML model for each unique group? The more variables involved in a ML model, the more chance it has for error. So this would decrease the number of variables a ML model would have to consider. But on the other hand, having a simple interface is much more easy to use. Perhaps there is a way to combine multiple ML models into one interface. Buolamwini provides us with an example of what this could look like in Unmasking AI. She happily reports, “But history has also shown us that alternative systems can be made. In the digital era, the LDK camera series developed by Philips explicitly handled skin tone variation with two chips—one for processing darker tones and another for processing lighter tones” (Buolamwini 44). Perhaps something analogous could happen here with our silverware model if we were to expand its audience.

      A challenge to making a model for everyone is that people disagree. Maybe this is obvious, but one person might consider a particular piece of metal as silverware and another person may not. If this is the case, the ML model needs to recognize the nuance.

      Dialog with Buolamwini’s book Unmasking AI and her concept of “the coded gaze” in relation to our ML Model

      At face value, designing a machine to recognize silverware carries no significant threat to humanity. However, the technology behind the machine drags a weighty load able to crush dreams and deny rights. In Buolamwini’s book Unmasking AI, the reader acquires a reasonable caution regarding the use of technology and the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in particular. What might seem as unharmful at first can turn into something lethal. Buolamwini tugs at readers to question the technology they use and not simply take it at face value. After depicting an example of the harmful effects of technology failure, she narrates, “I used these videos to tee up the idea that computer vision technology was further infiltrating our lives and that the consequences of not being detected by a computer vision system were not just for chuckles but could in fact be grave” (Buolamwini 37). Machine learning underlies both the silverware recognition model and Buolamwini’s “vision technology” example (Buolamwini 37).

      In particular, this assignment has demonstrated in a small way what Buolamwini calls the “coded gaze” (Buolamwini xiii) She defines it as follows: “[T]he coded gaze describes the ways in which the priorities, preferences, and prejudices of those who have the power to shape technology can propagate harm, such as discrimination and erasure. We can encode prejudice into technology even if it is not intentional” (Buolamwini xiii). In my experience with the background challenge, I could see how the test model took on the features of my own context. It helped me see how larger ML models can take on not only features but values and ideologies based on the data they are trained on.
